Palm oil mills: where does the fruit come from?

Under the EU Deforestation Regulation, anyone selling palm oil into Europe has to show it didn't come from land cleared after 2020. The oil is traceable to a mill, but mills buy fresh fruit from many farms around them. So I asked two questions: which land can realistically supply each mill, and is forest being cleared there?

I screened 361 mills in two very different provinces, West Kalimantan on Borneo and Riau on Sumatra, using only open data. This page explains how, what I found, and where the method stops.

How I did it

  1. Mills. I took every mill in the Universal Mill List and selected them by location inside each province, not by the province written in the list. That caught eight mills with the wrong province label.
  2. Where the palm is. A 10 m map of mature oil palm from 2021, split into industrial estates and smallholder farms.
  3. How far fruit can travel. Fruit has to be milled within about a day of harvest, so the driving part is short. I built a road network from OpenStreetMap with typical truck speeds and measured the drive from every palm area to every mill. Estate roads are often missing from OpenStreetMap, so I allowed up to 3 km off the mapped roads.
  4. Each mill's sourcing landscape. All palm a truck can reach within 2 hours of the mill. Most palm can reach several mills, so these areas overlap and I never add them up.
  5. Forest cleared since the cutoff. Forest at the end of 2020 from the EU's own reference map (JRC), and tree cover lost in 2021 to 2024 on that forest.
  6. Why the forest was cleared. Loss near palm isn't always palm. I used a global map of the dominant cause of forest loss to keep only agricultural clearing in the score, and to show mining and other causes separately.
  7. One number per mill. Agricultural clearing of 2020 forest in the mill's landscape, per 1,000 ha of palm it can reach. Dividing by palm keeps big central mills from ranking high just because their area is large.

Almost all palm is close to a mill, and usually to many

In both provinces about nine in ten hectares of palm are within an hour of a mill. What differs is competition. In West Kalimantan a typical plot can reach 7 mills within 2 hours. In Riau it's 31. That matters for traceability: when fruit can go to that many mills, knowing a mill's location tells you very little about which farms supplied it. Only plot-level data from the mill can close that gap.

The clearing front is in West Kalimantan

Using the same colour scale for both provinces, West Kalimantan has a clear agricultural clearing front through its west-central belt and along its eastern frontier. Riau is almost all green. Its remaining forest is small and mostly on peat or in protected areas, which fits a frontier that was largely cleared before 2020.

In West Kalimantan, mills with RSPO certification have lower scores (median 54 against 150 for the others), but certified mills also cluster in the south, where clearing is low anyway. In Riau there is no difference.

How I checked it

  • With my own eyes. I looked at Sentinel-2 images before and after for four of the places with the most clearing. All four were real clearing, but only one was a new palm plantation. One was mining, which is why I added the cause of loss.
  • The cause map, against those images. The global drivers map called the plantation agriculture and the pits mining, matching what the images show.
  • The off-road allowance. Ranking the West Kalimantan mills with 1 km instead of 3 km off the mapped roads gives almost the same order (rank correlation 0.99).
  • Against the usual 50 km circle. Many screening tools draw a 50 km circle around each mill. With circles instead of road travel time, the mills rank broadly the same way (rank correlation 0.86 in West Kalimantan, 0.92 in Riau), but about a quarter of them land in a different risk class (30% and 22%), and only 6 and 7 of the top 10 stay in the top 10. In West Kalimantan, where roads are sparse, the circles cover 1.7 times more land than a truck can reach in 2 hours.
  • Totals against known figures. The palm area for Riau (2.85 Mha) and the protected forest in West Kalimantan (3.7 Mha) are close to official numbers.

What this can't tell you

This is a screening of the landscape around each mill. It shows where a buyer should look first. It is not evidence of what any mill or company actually sourced, which is why mills appear only by their public ID.

  • The palm map is from 2021 and counts mature palm only, so newer and young plantings are missing. The mill list is from 2022.
  • The cause of forest loss comes from a 1 km map that gives the main cause over 2001 to 2025, not for each pixel or year. "Agriculture" there also includes crops other than palm.
  • Truck speeds come from road types, since speed limits are tagged on less than 1% of roads. Real trips depend on road condition and season.
  • Mills whose roads don't connect to the wider network, or with under 2,000 ha of palm in reach, are shown but not scored (6 in West Kalimantan, 8 in Riau).
  • Plantation concession maps and the global protected-areas database were left out on purpose: their licences don't allow this kind of use. I used Indonesia's own forest-area map for protected land instead.
