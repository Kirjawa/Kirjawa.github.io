How it was made: the question, data, method, checks and limits

1The question

Take the solar parks and wind farms mapped in OpenStreetMap in Bulgaria: 504 sites of 1 ha or more, 392 solar parks and 112 wind farms. How many sit in or near a sensitive location for nature, how does the answer move with the distance, and is the land around them changing?

This is the desk exercise behind the Locate phase of the TNFD's LEAP approach, and behind the metric that ESRS E4, GRI 101-5 and TNFD all ask for: sites in or near protected areas or areas of high biodiversity importance, with the distance stated. The same screening, with the same rules, is shown for Portugal. For a single project going to a lender, see the critical habitat pre-screening at Kaliakra.

Companies reporting under ESRS E4, GRI 101 or TNFD count their sites in or near sensitive locations for nature. The count is only as good as two things: the site list, and the rule for "near". TNFD doesn't fix a distance and GRI 101-5 asks for it to be stated, so the screening shows how the count moves with it.

Bulgaria is adding solar fast, and Natura 2000 covers about a third of its land. Between 2022 and 2024, solar capacity grew from 1,737 to 4,560 MW (Eurostat).

3Method in brief

  1. Sites. Solar parks and wind farms mapped as power plants in OpenStreetMap, plus the wind turbines outside them, grouped into a farm when they stand within 800 m of each other. That is the distance used for the global wind and solar farm datasets of Dunnett et al. (2020). A farm's footprint is the outline of its turbines plus 150 m. Sites under 1 ha are left out.
  2. Distances. Inside, and within 1 km for "near": close enough for construction, access roads and edge effects to reach a protected site. Wind farms are also checked within 5 km of Birds Directive sites, since birds range well beyond the sites that protect them.
  3. TNFD sensitive locations. Open data covers four of the five criteria. Important for biodiversity: Natura 2000 or likely critical habitat, inside or within 1 km. High ecosystem integrity: Biodiversity Intactness around the site in the country's top quarter. Rapid decline in integrity: a fall around the site from 2017 to 2020 steeper than on 95% of the country's land. High physical water risk: Aqueduct's physical risk, for water quantity or quality, high or extremely high. Baseline water stress, which ESRS E3 and GRI 303 use, is in the download too. The fifth, ecosystem services for Indigenous Peoples and local communities, needs local knowledge.
  4. Year by year. Within 1 km of each site, against land elsewhere in the country: tree cover lost each year, with its main cause, and changes between natural cover (trees, rangeland, wetland vegetation) and crops or built-up land. A change in the land cover maps counts only if it held: the same in 2017 and 2018, different in 2022 and 2023. A site's surroundings show a notable loss when at least 5% of that land lost natural or tree cover.

The rules and distances were written down before the results and not tuned to them.

4The sites

Most of the 504 sites are small solar parks: 392, half of them under 4.4 ha, spread across the lowlands. The 112 wind farms are concentrated in the north-east, around Kaliakra and Kavarna. 47% of the sites are in or within 1 km of Natura 2000 or likely critical habitat, 26% of the solar parks are in catchments with high physical water risk, and 317 sites meet at least one of the four TNFD criteria covered here.

5Is the site list complete?

The count is only as good as the list. OpenStreetMap shows what volunteers have mapped, and few solar parks carry their capacity there, so the list can't be checked against the official statistics. The list has 392 solar parks over 4,415 ha, against 4,560 MW of solar in Eurostat for 2024, rooftops included. Wind farms come to about 930 MW from their turbines' own tags and the median size for the rest, against 706 MW in Eurostat. Most turbines carry no capacity tag, so that is an estimate, and being above the official figure doesn't show that no wind farm is missing.

A way to check the list from satellite, finding solar parks from their Sentinel-2 signature, is shown on the Portugal page, on one area of Ribatejo.

6Year by year around the sites

Is the land around the sites losing natural cover? Two yearly records answer it for the land within 1 km of every site, counted once where sites are close together: tree cover lost each year from 2017 to 2025 with its main cause, and natural cover against crops and built-up land from 2017 to 2023 (the figure at the top).

  • Tree cover: little near the sites, and little lost: 0.3% of the land within 1 km from 2017 to 2025, against 0.7% in the countryside, mostly forestry.
  • Natural cover: scarcer near the sites, 26% of the land against 57% in the countryside, and it swaps with crops more often, but the net change from 2019 to 2022 was small: -0.09% of the land, against +0.14% in the countryside.
  • Site by site: 26 sites lost natural or tree cover over at least 5% of the land around them. The figures for every site, with the cause, are in the download.

A change near a site isn't necessarily caused by the site. These records show where the land is changing, which is what TNFD's "rapid decline in ecosystem integrity" asks about, and where a monitoring visit or a closer look would start.

8What open data can't tell here

A site counted here sits in or near a sensitive location by the stated rule and distance; what that means for the site is for the assessment to say.

  • Key biodiversity areas and species records, which sit behind licences that don't allow this use. The critical habitat layer stands in for them, at about 1 km resolution.
  • Ecosystem services for Indigenous Peoples and local communities, the fifth TNFD criterion.
  • Exact site boundaries. Grouping turbines can join or split wind farms, and an outline drawn around turbines is not the leased area. A client's own site register replaces all of this.
  • Recent change. Each layer has a date: Natura 2000 end 2024, Biodiversity Intactness up to 2020, WorldCover 2021, the annual land cover maps up to 2023.
  • Whether a change near a site was caused by it. And tree cover loss includes plantation harvests, which grow back.

9What the checks caught

Grouping turbines within 1.5 km joined the wind farms around Kavarna into one 6,000 ha site, so turbines 8 km from the bird area were counted with it. The map showed it, and the published 800 m replaced it. The first decline in integrity included each site's own footprint, so a park built between 2017 and 2020 showed its own construction as decline; now only the surroundings count. The first try at dating each park's construction from the annual land cover maps failed, because they read panels as rangeland or crops: the largest park, 622 ha, never showed as built.
