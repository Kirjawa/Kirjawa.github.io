Site screening for lender standards and nature disclosures

Demonstration on open data. Screening, not assessment, and not client work. A flag here means "look closer", never a finding about a site.

Take the solar parks and wind farms of 1 ha or more mapped in OpenStreetMap in Bulgaria and mainland Portugal: 1,237 sites. Which of them sit in or near a protected area or likely critical habitat, and which would a lender expect to go through a critical habitat assessment under IFC Performance Standard 6 or EBRD Performance Requirement 6?

The screening follows the Locate phase of the TNFD's LEAP approach. Its headline number is the one ESRS E4, GRI 101-5 and TNFD all ask for: sites in or near protected areas or areas of high biodiversity importance.

Kaliakra, on Bulgaria's Black Sea coast, and every screened site in both countries. Select the image to open it full size.

  • The headline metric: 238 of 504 sites in Bulgaria (47%) and 232 of 733 in Portugal (32%) are in or within 1 km of a Natura 2000 site or likely critical habitat.
  • For lenders: 121 sites in Bulgaria and 155 in Portugal are inside Natura 2000 or on likely critical habitat, where a critical habitat assessment is likely to be asked for. Another 117 and 77 are within 1 km, worth a check.
  • Wind and birds: 77 of Bulgaria's 112 wind farms and 59 of Portugal's 374 are within 5 km of a Birds Directive site.
  • Water: 645 of the sites in Portugal (88%) and 101 in Bulgaria (20%) are in catchments with high or extremely high physical water risk. Solar and wind use little water, but the criterion is about the place, not the plant.
  • A known case: Kaliakra, where in 2016 the EU Court of Justice ruled against Bulgaria over wind farms approved in an important bird area. There, 19 of the 21 wind sites within 1 km of the protected site are flagged for assessment, and the other 2 for a check.
  • The distance matters: in Bulgaria, 20% of the sites are inside Natura 2000, 47% within 1 km and 90% within 5 km. Every number here comes with its distance.
  • Year by year: in Portugal, 19% of the land within 1 km of the sites lost tree cover from 2017 to 2025, against 12% of the countryside, mostly to the fires of 2017 and 2025. In Bulgaria, natural cover around the sites barely changed overall.

1The question

Lenders that follow IFC Performance Standard 6 or EBRD Performance Requirement 6 ask early on whether a project is in or near critical habitat or a legally protected area. Companies reporting under ESRS E4, GRI 101 or TNFD count their sites in or near sensitive locations. Both start with the same desk exercise: every site against the same layers, with the distances stated.

Bulgaria and Portugal are adding solar fast, and both have large Natura 2000 networks. Between 2022 and 2024, solar capacity grew from 1,737 to 4,560 MW in Bulgaria and from 2,646 to 5,639 MW in Portugal (Eurostat).

3Method in brief

  1. Sites. Solar parks and wind farms mapped as power plants in OpenStreetMap, plus the wind turbines outside them, grouped into a farm when they stand within 800 m of each other. That is the distance used for the global wind and solar farm datasets of Dunnett et al. (2020). A farm's footprint is the outline of its turbines plus 150 m. Sites under 1 ha are left out, and only mainland Portugal is included.
  2. Distances. Inside, and within 1 km for "near": close enough for construction, access roads and edge effects to reach a protected site. Wind farms are also checked within 5 km of Birds Directive sites, since birds range well beyond the sites that protect them. TNFD doesn't fix a distance and GRI 101-5 asks for it to be stated, so the checks show how the numbers move with it.
  3. TNFD sensitive locations. Open data covers four of the five criteria. Important for biodiversity: Natura 2000 or likely critical habitat, inside or within 1 km. High ecosystem integrity: Biodiversity Intactness around the site in the country's top quarter. Rapid decline in integrity: a fall around the site from 2017 to 2020 steeper than on 95% of the country's land. High physical water risk: Aqueduct's physical risk, for water quantity or quality, high or extremely high. Baseline water stress, which ESRS E3 and GRI 303 use, is in the download too. The fifth, ecosystem services for Indigenous Peoples and local communities, needs local knowledge.
  4. Lender screening. "Assessment likely": inside Natura 2000 or on likely critical habitat. "Check": within 1 km of either. Potential critical habitat is reported but not flagged: it covers 397 of Bulgaria's 504 sites, so it doesn't tell one site from another.
  5. Priority locations. The sites flagged "Assessment likely", plus wind farms within 5 km of a Birds Directive site: 156 in Bulgaria and 183 in Portugal.
  6. Year by year. Within 1 km of each site, and on 400 random 2 km squares of each country for comparison: tree cover lost each year, with its main cause, and changes between natural cover (trees, rangeland, wetland vegetation) and crops or built-up land. A change in the land cover maps counts only if it held: the same in 2017 and 2018, different in 2022 and 2023. A site's surroundings show a notable loss when at least 5% of that land lost natural or tree cover.

The rules and distances were written down before the results and not tuned to them.

4Bulgaria and Portugal

Bulgaria's 504 sites are mostly small solar parks (392, half of them under 4.4 ha), and its wind farms are concentrated in the north-east, around Kaliakra. Natura 2000 covers a large share of the country, so "near" is common: 47% of the sites are in or within 1 km of Natura 2000 or likely critical habitat. 5Year by year around the sites

Is the land around the sites losing natural cover? Two yearly records answer it for the land within 1 km of every site, counted once where sites are close together, and for 400 random 2 km squares of each country as a comparison: tree cover lost each year from 2017 to 2025 with its main cause, and natural cover against crops and built-up land from 2017 to 2023.

Near the sites against the countryside, year by year. Select the image to open it full size.

  • Portugal: 19.1% of the land near the sites lost tree cover from 2017 to 2025, against 12.0% of the countryside. Most of it burned, in 2017 and 2025 above all: wildfire alone took 14.0%, against 7.6%. Forestry runs at about 0.5% a year, as in the countryside. Most wind farms are in hill country of grass, shrub and forest, so fire is a risk the sites share with the land around them.
  • Bulgaria: little tree cover near the sites, and little lost (0.3% since 2017, against 0.7% in the countryside, mostly forestry). Natural cover is scarcer near the sites, 26% of the land against 57%, and it swaps with crops more often, but the net change from 2019 to 2022 was small: -0.09% of the land, against +0.14% in the countryside.
  • Site by site: 26 sites in Bulgaria and 514 in Portugal lost natural or tree cover over at least 5% of the land around them. In Portugal, wildfire is the main cause for 252 of them. The figures for every site, with the cause, are in the download.

Before, after and since: four wind farms in central Portugal whose surroundings burned in 2017. Select the image to open it full size.

A change near a site isn't necessarily caused by the site. These records show where the land is changing, which is what TNFD's "rapid decline in ecosystem integrity" asks about, and where a monitoring visit or a closer look would start.

Screening is not assessment. A flag says where to look closer. Whether a site is critical habitat is decided by the assessment, with species data and field survey.

7From a one-off screening to daily monitoring

Run once, a screening is a snapshot. Run as a service, it keeps up with the sites: a new or changed site triggers the screening, the reference layers are refreshed once a year, and satellite data watch each site and its surroundings every few days.

How the screening would run at scale, with daily monitoring. Select the image to open it full size.

  • Small chips, not whole scenes. Each site is watched through its footprint and buffer only, so thousands of sites stay cheap to follow.
  • Alerts are triaged before anyone sees them: weather filters for frost, snow and drought, an optical view as the referee for radar alerts, and known causes such as harvest or mowing, as in the habitat pre-map sample.
  • A person reviews a blind sample before anything reaches a client, and reviewed alerts feed back into the rules.
  • Licences are kept per layer. A client with an IBAT licence can add WDPA and key biodiversity areas without changing the rest.

8What open data can't tell here

A coarse layer, a boundary that depends on a rule, and layers of different ages. Select the image to open it full size.

  • Key biodiversity areas and species records, which sit behind licences that don't allow this use. The critical habitat layer stands in for them, at about 1 km resolution.
  • Whether the species that would make a site critical habitat are there. That needs species data and field survey.
  • Ecosystem services for Indigenous Peoples and local communities, the fifth TNFD criterion.
  • Exact site boundaries. OpenStreetMap shows what has been mapped. Grouping turbines can join or split wind farms, and an outline drawn around turbines is not the leased area. A client's own site register replaces all of this.
  • Recent change. Each layer has a date: Natura 2000 end 2024, Biodiversity Intactness up to 2020, WorldCover 2021, the annual land cover maps up to 2023.
  • A park's own construction. The annual land cover maps read solar panels as rangeland or crops, so they can't date when a park was built. And tree cover loss includes plantation harvests, which grow back.
  • The final call, which belongs to the assessment.

9How AI was used

An agent (Claude Code) wrote the scripts: the site list from OpenStreetMap, the downloads from each open service, the screening, the year-by-year record, the checks, the maps, the memos and the drawing. The rules were written down before the results, and the results were checked against a known case, the official statistics and the maps themselves.

What it got wrong, and what caught it: grouping turbines within 1.5 km joined the wind farms around Kavarna into one 6,000 ha site, so turbines 8 km from the bird area were flagged along with it. The map showed it, and the published 800 m replaced it. The first screening flagged almost every site in Bulgaria, because potential critical habitat covers most of the lowlands; it is now reported, not flagged. The first decline in integrity included each site's own footprint, so a park built between 2017 and 2020 showed its own construction as decline; now only the surroundings count. The first water criterion used Aqueduct's overall score, which also counts regulatory and reputational risk; TNFD asks about physical water risk, and with it 645 sites in Portugal qualify instead of 130. The first try at dating each park's construction from the annual land cover maps failed, because they read panels as rangeland or crops; the yearly step looks at the surroundings instead. And the first satellite view of Kaliakra covered only a strip at the bottom of the map, because the area spans four Sentinel-2 tiles.
