Monitoring a solar park between survey visits

Demonstration on open data. Not a site visit, not a compliance assessment, and not client work. Satellite data shows where and when something changed; the ecologists and the habitat steering group judge what it means.

Cleve Hill Solar Park, on the Graveney Marshes in Kent, was built from April 2023 and has run since 1 July 2025: 373 MW and more than 550,000 modules. Its consent came with a habitat management area of about 56 ha for the brent geese, lapwing and golden plover of The Swale next door, and with ecologists' visits each May and late summer.

Between those visits, what can open satellite data show?

Before and after, with the mapped arrays and the habitat management area. Select the image to open it full size.

  • Construction, dated: half the arrays had steel by June 2024 and modules by October 2024. On 8 June 2024 Sentinel-2 saw modules on 7.3% of the arrays; the project's newsletter gave 7.4% of modules installed that day.
  • The arrays: 202 ha mapped from Sentinel-2, covering 96% of the panel blocks drawn in OpenStreetMap.
  • The habitat area: about 56 ha of arable land, cultivated and sown in August and September 2023 and green from late October, through the first winter of construction, as the plan intended.
  • Management signals: sharp falls in greenness at the end of May 2025 and in early June 2026; none from August to October, before the birds arrive.
  • Water: no standing water seen on the habitat area in the two winters since the wetland works, by radar or by Sentinel-2.
  • For the next visit: four things to check, below, including the sward height before winter, which satellite data can't measure.

1The question

Solar parks and habitat banks now come with long monitoring duties. At Cleve Hill, the outline Landscape and Biodiversity Management Plan asks for an ecologist's visit in May and in August or September in years 1, 2, 3, 5, 10 and 20, and winter bird counts twice a month. Between visits, the steering group of Natural England, Kent Wildlife Trust, the RSPB and the Environment Agency relies on reports.

The plan's commitments for the habitat management area are specific: grassland sown in autumn before the first winter of construction, summer grazing or an early autumn cut so that the sward is under 100 mm before winter, raised water levels, and at least 50.1 ha of land more than 50 m from modules, transformers, the flood bank and roads. Several of these leave a trace in satellite data.

3Method in brief

  1. Arrays. Modules are dark in near infrared and bright in shortwave infrared, so a moisture index (NDMI) turns strongly negative over them, about -0.62 against +0.05 for the grass around. Since operation began, pixels whose median stays below -0.30 are arrays.
  2. Construction dates. Sentinel-2: the first clear view from which a pixel keeps the module signature. Sentinel-1: the steel frames raise the radar return by 3 to 5 dB before the modules send it away; the steel date is the first rise of 3 dB that lasts three passes, on 100 m cells so that radar speckle averages out.
  3. The habitat area. Land east of the compound that the Crop Map of England shows as arable in 2020 to 2022 and as grass in 2024, with the plan's 50 m exclusions drawn from the mapped arrays, the substations, the Environment Agency's flood defences and the roads. The agreed boundary is the steering group's; this is an approximation for monitoring.
  4. Its record. Every clear view since 2017: greenness (NDVI) and bare soil, strict enough that grass browned by drought doesn't count. Management signals: falls in NDVI of more than 0.2 between clear views on at least a quarter of the area, the rule of Kolecka et al. (2018). Winter water: very dark radar in both polarisations, with frost and snow passes skipped, and Sentinel-2 as the referee.

4Construction, dated

When modules appeared on each part of the arrays, and the share with steel and modules over time. Select the image to open it full size.

The radar record has two steps: the steel frames raise it, then the modules, like mirrors, send the signal away, 4 to 5 dB below the level before construction. Modules followed the steel by a median of 137 days. 95% of the arrays had modules by February 2025, three months before commissioning in May.

5The habitat management area

The habitat area year by year, and where to look on the next visit. Select the image to open it full size.

  • From arable to grassland. The last crop came off in July 2023. Bare soil from August to mid-September, then green from late October. Every winter before showed bare soil; none since.
  • Bare patches in August and September 2024, on 11.9 ha, fit the scrape and swale works. Almost none in 2025. 3.2 ha in September and October 2026.
  • Management signals. Greenness fell sharply on 29 May 2025 (on 78% of the area) and on 6 June 2026 (on 38%). Both springs were dry, so this may be a cut, heavy grazing or quick browning. No fall showed from August to October in any year.
  • The plan's 50 m exclusions take 1.7 ha of the approximate area, 3%: mostly road edges, and 0.2 ha near the arrays and transformers.
  • Water. Never more than half a hectare of open water on any radar pass in any winter, and none that Sentinel-2 confirmed after the works. Seasonal flooding, if it happens, is in features too small or too brief to see, or not happening.

6What to check on the next visit

  • Sward height before winter (the plan: under 100 mm). No cut or sharp fall showed from August to October in 2024, 2025 or 2026, and grazing at a few sheep per hectare doesn't show from space.
  • The falls of late May 2025 and early June 2026: a cut, heavy grazing or quick browning? And how do they fit the breeding season of ground-nesting birds, which the site's ecologists recorded in 2024?
  • Bare ground in September and October 2026 (3.2 ha, on the map): works, poaching, reseeding or drought?
  • Water in ditches and scrapes. Neither sensor saw standing water on the area in the last two winters. Are the water control measures holding water as intended?

This is the shape of a note for the steering group between visits: what changed, where, and what to look at. It doesn't replace the visit or the bird counts.

8What satellite data can't tell here

  • Sward height and structure, the plan's main target for the wintering birds. Greenness is not height.
  • Grazing at a few sheep per hectare, which changes the sward too gradually to show as an event.
  • Bird use, which needs the winter counts; and species and condition, which need the survey.
  • The ground under the modules. At about 70% cover, Sentinel-2 sees the modules, not the grass between and beneath them.
  • Small or short-lived water: scrapes smaller than a few 10 m pixels, or flooding that drains between passes.

9How AI was used

An agent (Claude Code) wrote the scripts: the downloads, the array map, the construction dates, the habitat area's record, the figures and the note. Every result was checked against something independent: OpenStreetMap's panel blocks, the project's published progress and dates, the Crop Map of England and the plan's figures.

What it got wrong, and what caught it: it expected the radar to brighten over the arrays; once the modules are on, it darkens, and the record showed it. Dated pixel by pixel, the radar put the modules months late, because single pixels are too noisy; 100 m cells fixed the steel dates, and Sentinel-2 dates the modules. Its first habitat area took all the unfenced land in the east, 93 ha, until the crop map narrowed it to the arable that was reverted, 56 ha, as the plan says. And it counted the snow of February 2018 as water.
